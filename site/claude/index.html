Chapter 02 · The basics

Claude is a thinking partner, not a magic answer engine.

It is strongest when you give it a real brief and review its work before anything important moves. Everything else in this walkthrough is built on that one idea.

P · 01

Designed to be helpful and steerable.

Claude comes from Anthropic. The practical point is that it is designed to act more like a careful coworker than a blank chatbot, and you can steer the tone, the rules, and the output shape.

P · 02

Best at knowledge work.

Drafting. Summarizing. Comparing. Explaining. Organizing. The parts of the day that are text-heavy, document-heavy, follow-up-heavy. Not the parts that need handshakes and judgment.

P · 03

You still own the decision.

Claude prepares the work. You review, you decide, you sign off. The review point is the value, not a bug to be automated away.

Chapter 05 · Fit & limits

Where Claude fits. Where it stops.

There's a simple split that handles 90% of "should I use AI here?" questions. It's not about smart vs. dumb work. It's about repetition and review.

Strong fit

When the work is text-heavy and easy to review.

  • Drafting · emails, summaries, status notes, recap reports.
  • Extracting · pulling the key details out of long documents.
  • Organizing · turning scattered notes into a clean next-step brief.
  • Comparing · options, vendors, policies, workflows.
  • Building reusable pieces · SOPs, simple dashboards, explainers.

Still needs you

When judgment, relationships, or compliance are at stake.

  • Final approval on sensitive client communication.
  • Verification of dates, numbers, contract details, regulated language.
  • Sharing decisions — what gets surfaced, what doesn't.
  • The stop button · deciding when automation should hand off to a person.
  • Owning the relationship — judgment and accountability stay human.

The working rule, full size

Claude prepares. The realtor, brokerage staff member, or office lead reviews. The human stays accountable.

Chapter 07 · Your first move

If this is worth trying, start small and visible.

Don't roll Claude out everywhere. Pick one repetitive workflow, give it a real brief, keep the review point visible, and judge by the time saved.

  1. 01

    Pick one recurring workflow.

    Choose the thing people already complain about: document setup, follow-up emails, reminders, summaries, inbox cleanup. Repetitive and reviewable beats clever and rare.

  2. 02

    Give Claude a real brief.

    Tell it who this is for, what the outcome should be, what tone fits, and what a good answer looks like. The template below is a starting point.

  3. 03

    Keep the review point visible.

    Mark — out loud, in the workflow — where a person checks the output before anything important is sent, signed, or approved.

  4. 04

    Expand only after the first win is clear.

    When the same workflow keeps coming back and the time saved is obvious, that's the signal to add a project, a saved skill, or a connected tool.

Take this with you

A starter brief you can paste into Claude today.

Edit the bracketed parts. The structure does most of the work.

starter-brief.txt 
You are helping me with [the recurring task: e.g. drafting a client follow-up].

Context:
- The audience is [who reads this, and what they care about].
- This is for [the situation — meeting, transaction, file, decision].
- My constraints are [length, tone, format, deadline].
- Source material is below — use it as the source of truth.

Task:
- Produce a first draft that I can review and edit.
- Highlight anything you weren't sure about so I can check it.
- End with [the one thing I'll decide before sending].

Source material:
[paste notes, document, or thread here]

After you paste this, watch what happens when you tighten the bracketed parts. That's the lever, every time.

Bottom line

The right question isn't "what can AI do?"

It's which part of the work repeats every week, and how do we keep review in place? If you have an honest answer to that, you already know where Claude fits — and where it doesn't.
